    Handout Section 1.8: Transformations of Functions
    

    Vertical Translation of a Function.

    The transformation \(y=g(x)=f(x)+a\) is a vertical translation of the graph of \(f\text{.}\) That is, every point \((x,f(x))\) on the graph of \(f\) gets shifted vertically to the corresponding point \((x,f(x)+a)\) on the graph of \(g\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    That is, \(f(x)+a\) shifts \(f(x)\) by .
    🔗

    Horizontal Translation of a Function.

    The transformation \(y=h(x)=f(x-b)\) is a horizontal translation of the graph of \(f\text{.}\) That is, every point \((x,f(x))\) on the graph of \(f\) gets shifted horizontally to the corresponding point \((x+b,f(x))\) on the graph of \(h\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    That is, \(f(x-b)\) shifts \(f(x)\) by . **It’s opposite the direction you probably think it moves! Ex: \(f(x-2)\) moves right by 2!**
    🔗
    The graph on the left shows the vertical translation of the function \(y=f(x)=|x|\text{,}\) it is shifted vertically downward by 3, so it becomes the graph \(g(x) = f(x) - 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The graph on the right shows the horizontal translation of a function \(f(x)\) (given by a piecewise linear equation). Since \(f\) is shifted horizontally to the right by \(2\text{,}\) so it becomes the graph \(h(x) = f(x-2)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Vertical Scaling of a Function.

    The transformation \(y=v(x)=cf(x)\) is a vertical stretch of the graph of \(f\text{.}\) Every point \((x,f(x))\) on the graph of \(f\) gets stretched vertically to the corresponding point \((x,cf(x))\) on the graph of \(v\text{.}\) If \(0<c<1\text{,}\) the graph of \(v\) is a compression of \(f\) toward the \(x\)-axis. If \(c>1\text{,}\) the graph of \(v\) is a stretch of \(f\) away from the \(x\)-axis. If \(c<0\text{,}\) the transformed function \(y=v(x)=cf(x)\) is a reflection of the graph of \(f\) across the \(x\)-axis followed by a vertical stretch by a factor of \(|c|\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Combining Functions (Section 1.9)
    🔗
    Note we can also combine two functions by adding, subtracting, multiplying, or even dividing functions, and this will create a new function. For instance if \(f(x) = 3x + 1\) and \(g(x) = 2x - 4\text{,}\) then we can take a new function \(h(x)\) to be the sum of \(f\) and \(g\text{,}\) so
    \begin{equation*} h(x) = f(x) + g(x) = (3x + 1) + (2x - 4) = 5x - 3. \end{equation*}
    We can also subtract, multiply, and divide. For example we may choose to make a new function \(k(x) = \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\text{,}\) which we could simplify in this case to
    \begin{equation*} k(x) = \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{3x+1}{2x-4}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
