    Definition 4.2.2: Tangent.
    \begin{align*} \tan(\theta) \amp =\frac{\sin(\theta)}{\cos(\theta)}, \\ \amp \text{if } \cos(\theta)\ne 0 \end{align*}

    Properties of the tangent function: \(h(t)=\tan(t)\).

    • Domain: all real numbers except \({t=\frac{\pi}{2}\pm k\pi}\) where \(k\) is any whole number;
      🔗

    • Range: all real numbers;
      🔗
      🔗
    • Period: \(\pi\text{;}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • it is increasing on any interval on which the function is defined at every point in the interval.

    Activity 131 Activity 4.2.2.

    The top of a 225 foot tower is to be anchored by four cables that each make an angle of \(32.5^{\circ}\) with the ground. How long do the cables have to be and how far from the base of the tower must they be anchored?
    🔗
    Solution.
    We only really need to consider one anchor. That anchor will go from the top of the tower down to the ground, and form an angle of \(32.5^\circ\) with the ground. So our triangle has a vertical side corresponding to the tower (straight up and down) of 225 ft, a horizontal edge on the ground that I will call \(x\) ft, and the hypotenuse of the triangle is the length of the cable which I will call \(L\text{.}\) So the ground is adjacent to the angle \(32.5^\circ\text{,}\) and that angle is touching the ground (not up in the air).
    🔗
    We can find \(x\) or \(L\) first, it doesn’t matter which, but we want both. Notice
    \begin{equation*} \tan(32.5)=\frac{225}{x} \end{equation*}
    which we can rearrange to \(x\tan(32.5)=225\text{,}\) and one more time to get \({x=\frac{225}{\tan(32.5)}}\text{.}\) That’s an exact answer, which turns out to be approximately \(353.18\) ft.
    🔗
    There are many ways to find \(L\text{.}\) One of the cleanest is to use sine, since that will use information we were given instead of information we had to find (on the off chance we made a mistake it’s a tiny bit “safer”). So
    \begin{equation*} \sin(32.5)=\frac{225}{L} \end{equation*}
    which can be rearranged as \({L\sin(32.5)=225}\) and finally \({L=\frac{225}{\sin(32.5)}}\) feet (exact) or approximately \(418.76\)ft is the length of one cable.
    🔗
    🔗

    Activity 132 Activity 4.2.3.

    “Supertall” high rises have changed the Manhattan skyline. These skyscrapers are known for their small footprint in proportion to their height, with their ratio of width to height at most \(1:10\text{,}\) and some as extreme as \(1:25\text{.}\) Suppose that a relatively short “supertall” has been built to a height of 635 feet, as pictured in the figure, and that a second “supertall” is built nearby. Given the two angles that are computed from the new building, how tall, \(s\text{,}\) is the new building, and how far apart, \(d\text{,}\) are the two towers?
    🔗
    Solution.
    There are two right triangles here, but the one with the \(31^\circ\) angle has more info, so start with that one. We know the side opposite the angle, and adjacent, so let’s use tangent.
    \begin{equation*} \tan(31)=\frac{635}{d} \end{equation*}
    which rearranged goes to \(d\tan(31)=635\text{,}\) or \({d=]frac{635}{\tan(31)}}\) feet. (Approximately 1056.82 ft)
    🔗
    Now we know \(d\) we have enough info to use the other right triangle:
    \begin{equation*} \tan(34) = \frac{d}{s}=\frac{635/\tan(31)}{s} \end{equation*}
    which rearranged gives \({s \tan(34) = \frac{635}{\tan(31)}}\text{,}\) which rearranged one more time: \(s = \frac{635}{\tan(34)\tan(31)}\) feet. (Approximately 1566.80 ft.)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Activity 133 Activity 4.2.4.

    Surveyors are trying to determine the height of a hill relative to sea level. First, they choose a point to take an initial measurement with a sextant that shows the angle of elevation from the ground to the peak of the hill is \(19^{\circ}\text{.}\) Next, they move 1000 feet closer to the hill, staying at the same elevation relative to sea level, and find that the angle of elevation has increased to \(25^{\circ}\text{,}\) as pictured in the figure. We let \(h\) represent the height of the hill relative to the two measurements, and \(x\) represent the distance from the second measurement location to the “center” of the hill that lies directly under the peak.
    🔗

    (a)

    Using the right triangle with the \(25^{\circ}\) angle, find an equation that relates \(x\) and \(h\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\tan(25^\circ) = \frac{h}{x}\text{.}\) That’s it.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Using the right triangle with the \(19^{\circ}\) angle, find a second equation that relates \(x\) and \(h\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\tan(19^\circ) = \frac{h}{1000+x}\text{.}\) Also it.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Our work in (a) and (b) results in a system of two equations in the two unknowns \(x\) and \(h\text{.}\) Solve each of the two equations for \(h\) and then substitute appropriately in order to find a single equation in the variable \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(h = x\tan(25^\circ)\) and \(h=(1000+x)\tan(19^\circ)\text{.}\) Since \(h\) is the same, we can substitute one \(h\) for the other and we have the equation
    \begin{equation*} x\tan(25^\circ) = (1000+x)\tan(19^\circ). \end{equation*}
    This is one equation with \(x\) as the only variable.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Solve the equation from (c) to find the exact value of \(x\) and determine an approximate value accurate to 3 decimal places.
    🔗
    Solution.
    If I asked you to solve \({5x = (1000+x)(3)}\text{,}\) you would distribute to get \({5x=3000+3x}\text{,}\) move the \(x\)’s to one side \({2x=3000}\text{,}\) then get \({x=1500}\text{.}\) This is exactly the same equation as
    \begin{equation*} x\tan(25^\circ) = (1000+x)\tan(19^\circ) \end{equation*}
    just with different numbers. We can solve it the same way. Distribute:
    \begin{equation*} x\tan(25^\circ) = 1000\tan(19^\circ) + x\tan(19^\circ) \end{equation*}
    then get the x’s to one side:
    \begin{equation*} x\tan(25^\circ) - x\tan(19^\circ) = 1000\tan(19^\circ) \end{equation*}
    ah well we can’t combine the two tangents since they are evaluated at different angles, but we can factor out an \(x\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} x(\tan(25^\circ) - \tan(19^\circ)) = 1000\tan(19^\circ) \end{equation*}
    and now \(x\) is multiplied by that giant thing which is just a number, so we can divide by that number and get
    \begin{equation*} x = \frac{1000\tan(19^\circ)}{(\tan(25^\circ) - \tan(19^\circ))}. \end{equation*}
    Is doesn’t simplify, but it is exact. It is approximately 2822.819 feet.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    Use your preceding work to solve for \(h\) exactly, plus determine an estimate accurate to 3 decimal places.
    🔗
    Solution.
    We already have \(h=x\tan(25^\circ)\text{,}\) plug in \(x\text{:}\) \({h = \frac{1000\tan(19^\circ)}{(\tan(25^\circ) - \tan(19^\circ))}\tan(25^\circ)}\) which is approximately 1316.302 feet.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    If the surveyors’ initial measurements were taken from an elevation of 78 feet above sea level, how high above sea level is the peak of the hill?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(h+78\approx 1394\) feet.
